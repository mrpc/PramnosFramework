## The rule editor offers templates and lists, and wears the theme

### Changed

- The email action's **template** and **unsubscribe list** are choices in the rule editor: the
  mail templates by title, and the declared mail types' lists (FW-234).
- **`Rules\Actions::templates($options, $fill)`**: an application whose templates are not the
  framework's `mailtemplates` says which it has and fills one for a person.
- The rule editor's controls take the theme's field classes, and its rows line up.
- An application's template fill that returns `'emailtemplate' => ''` is sent bare, not wrapped
  again in the installation's email layout.

### Fixed

- The rule page's forms carry the session's token where the write-action contract looks for it.

### Documentation

- Audience guide: Templates, under Actions.

## Person events are kept in the change log

### Changed

- Each occurrence of a person event is written to the change log's application events
  (`pramnos.changelog_events`, entity `user`) through the write spool, instead of a table of its
  own: one history of what people did, partitioned, compressed and kept two years on TimescaleDB.
  `person_events`, the per-person summary, stays (FW-234 B).
- `create_person_events_tables` creates `person_events` only; `audience.event_log_days` is gone.
- `audience:prune-events` prunes the rule run log and segment stays that ended more than
  `audience.segment_history_days` ago (365), and says so in one line when there is nothing to do.
- *Had it been on* for an event-triggered rule reads the summary.

### Added

- An index on `changelog_events (entity, event, created_at)` (`add_event_index_to_changelog_events`,
  `changelog` feature).

### What changes for an application

- Windowed conditions ("N times in the last M days") and A/B goals need the `changelog` feature;
  without it they are refused or shown as not counted. A `person_event_log` made by yesterday's
  migration is no longer written and can be dropped.

### Documentation

- Audience guide: *The summary, and the change log*; Console guide; Upgrade Guide row.

## A/B tests

### Added

- `Pramnos\Audience\Experiments`: `variant($slug)` gives a person or visitor one variant by share,

- `/admin/Experiments` (ability `admin.experiments`) in the three themes: the list, the form, the

- A filter condition on a test's variant, and a rule split that follows a test.
- Migration `create_experiments_tables` (core).

### Documentation

- Audience guide: *A/B tests*.

## Person events drain where the change log's feature is off

### Fixed

- `ChangelogWriter::event()` encodes `details` when it spools the row. A drain in a process where
  the changelog provider never booted — an application with `changelog_events` and the feature
  off — had no transformer, and the column refused the array: every person event was kept back
  for the next run, so windowed conditions and A/B goals counted nobody (FW-237).
- The A/B results' footnote escapes its `95%`, which `sprintf` read as a placeholder and printed a
  literal `%s` for the visitors.

## The audience screens, redesigned

### Changed

- Segments, consent lists, rules and A/B tests share one design language in every theme: a page
  head with its actions, cards, tabs with counts and a search, tables whose row is one link,
  coloured status pills and ▲/▼ deltas, links to what uses a segment, empty states with a way in,
  a segmented control for a rule's status, and a footer that keeps a long form's buttons in
  reach (FW-234, design review round 1).
- **The builder**: a condition on one line; what it is about picked from a searchable list under
  the attributes' and events' headings; the word between rows switches *all*/*any*; the live
  panel sticks beside the conditions, with the breakdown as bars and each person of the sample
  with initials and a line under the name.
- **The rule editor**: when, who, what and until as numbered cards that fold to a one-line
  summary; the steps as cards joined by a line, added from a menu of kinds; events picked from the
  searchable list; sending hours on the 24-hour clock; limits as "at most [1] [email] per [1]
  days". A new step follows the last one that ends. The rule's page draws the same cards with how
  many went through each.
- `AudienceBuilder::classes()` returns the screens' own classes in every theme.

### Added

- `Attributes::sampleLine()` and `Filter::lines()`: what a sample shows under each name.
- `Segments::uses()` and `Rules::linksUsingSegment()`: what uses a segment, with links.
- `data-pf-combobox` (pf-audience.js) and `data-pf-filter-rows` (pf-utils.js) for any form.
- `Icon` names for the audience screens: plus, close, search, chevron, segment, rule,
  experiment, consent, clock, branch, split, goal, action, play, pause, shadow, stop.

### Documentation

- Audience guide: the screens' design, the two form pieces, the sample's line.

## The audience screens, second review

### Changed

- A sample's line says a date as "today", "yesterday", "5 days ago" or its day, and an empty one
  as "never" — never a raw Unix time.
- The words the screens show are their own phrases ("Group of conditions", "Rule status", "Goal
  of the test", "Test variant"…), not bare one-word keys an application may already translate
  in another sense.
- One of a thing is said in the singular: "1 step", "1 condition", "1 day".
- A step is named by its place and what it does ("2. Send an email") in the summaries, the
  editor's choices, the rule's page, its log and its test send — never by its id.
- A condition's row holds its fields at their own width with its remove button beside them; an
  empty filter says it is everybody; the live panel breaks down by the first list attribute at
  once; the end of the sending hours reads 21:00.
- The segment and group badges are equally legible, each with a border.

## Event transports, and events:status

### Added

- A queued listener's events go by a **transport**: `Pramnos\Event\Transport\EventTransport`,
  with the queue (`QueueTransport`) as every installation's and the default. `'events' =>
  ['transport' => …, 'listeners' => [Class => …]]` in app.php chooses one for the installation
  and per listener; one that is unknown or cannot carry events falls back to the queue, logged
  once (FW-235 P1). Nothing changes for an installation that sets nothing.
- `bin/pramnos events:status`: each queued listener's transport as set and as it would go — a
  dry run, with `--json`.
- `Event::events()`: the names something listens for.

### Documentation

- Queue guide: *Which transport carries a listener's events*; Console guide: `events:status`.

## The event stream, on the database

### Added

- The `stream` transport: an event is one entry in `event_log`, written in the caller's
  transaction, for every queued listener set to it; each listener is a consumer group reading from
  its own position (`event_consumers`). One row per event instead of one task per listener
  (FW-235 P2).
- `bin/pramnos events:consume`: the daemon that reads the groups — one consumer per group by lease;
  failures retried with a growing wait and kept as dead past the listener's attempts
  (`event_deliveries`); folded listeners handed to the queue, so they fold as before; idempotency
  keys as on the queue.
- `bin/pramnos events:prune` (scheduled daily): entries past `events.log_days` (30) that every
  group has read.
- Migration `create_event_log_tables` (`queue` feature). `event_log` and `event_deliveries` are
  declared personal data.
- `QueuedEvents::payload()` and `QueuedEvents::arguments()`: what travels for a firing, and the
  arguments built again — shared by the queue and the stream.

### Documentation

- Queue guide: *The stream: one entry per event*; Console guide: `events:consume`, `events:prune`.

### Fixed

### Documentation

## The event stream on Redis Streams

### Added

- `'events' => ['stream' => 'redis', 'redis' => [...], 'max_length' => 100000]`: the stream's
  groups read a Redis stream. `event_log` stays the outbox, written in the caller's transaction; a
  relay appends what is committed, at once outside a transaction and on every pass of
  `events:consume`, so nothing is lost between the commit and Redis. Consumer groups with
  `XREADGROUP`/`XACK`; what was read and never acknowledged is claimed again once it has waited;
  dead entries go to `event_deliveries` as on the database (FW-235 P3).
- A Redis whose `maxmemory-policy` is `allkeys-*` is refused for the queue, asked through
  `CONFIG GET` or, where that is disabled, `INFO memory`.
- On the Queue screen, a group's name is its class, the namespace beside it; with no consumer
  holding any group in the last minute, the card says no `events:consume` daemon is reading.

### Documentation

- Queue guide: *On Redis Streams*, and how an application adds a transport of its own — Kafka
  among them — with `Transports::register()` (FW-235 P5: the extension point, documented; a Kafka
  transport comes when an application's producer is brought into the framework).
