## Conversations: a person the reader sees anyway has a profile

### Changed

- `People::profile()` and `person/{id}` answer for somebody who may not use the conversations
  when the reader sees them anyway: they are in a conversation the reader reads — history brought
  in, an account whose access ended — or the reader reads them all. Such a profile has
  `canMessage` false and no direct conversation. A stranger is still null, and 404.
- Every profile carries `canMessage`: false for the reader themselves too.

### Documentation

- Conversations guide: *The page and the dock* (a person, as a client shows them), *The HTTP API*.

## Conversations: the dock and a basic page; the full page is the application's

### Changed

- The framework draws the dock over every page and a basic page at `/admin/Conversations`; a full
  chat application — a sidebar in sections, threads and activity views, a quick switcher, its own
  window and install, the beacon's browser client — is the application's to build on the JSON API,
  which keeps every action it needs.
- The dock's list is its own: the conversations newest first, a filter, a new conversation, the
  channels to join, files and space, the application's links, and the faces above the button.
- The basic page (`pf-conversations-basic.js`, `Client::basic()`) is plain JavaScript with no build:
  the list with what is unread, one conversation, earlier messages, a box to write in, live or read
  again every few seconds. An example of the API, dressed by the theme's own classes.

### Removed

- From `pf-conversations.js`: the page, the sidebar, the inbox views, the switcher, the own window
  and install, the beacon's browser client; their logic helpers and tests.
- The actions `manifest` and `worker`, `resources/js/conversations/sw.js`, and `Client::props()`'s
  `app`. `Client::app()` stays, for an application's own manifest.
- The `pf-conv-*` rules nothing draws any more, from each theme's `components.css`; the words only
  they showed, from `el.php`.

### What breaks in subclasses

- A subclass of `Controllers\Conversations` that calls or overrides `manifest()` or `worker()`:
  serve them from the application's own controller; the base class no longer has them.

### Documentation

- Conversations guide: *The dock and the basic page*, *When the server is down — the beacon*.
- Upgrade Guide: the full page is the application's.

## Moderation: bans, mutes, kicks, slow mode, a rate, a filter, reports and a log

### Added

- The `moderation` feature (`Pramnos\Moderation`), for anything people write, in scopes the
  application names:
  - bans and mutes of a subject — an account, a visitor, an address or a nickname — in a scope or
    everywhere, for a while or for good, and lifting them;
  - kicks, told as `moderation.kicked`;
  - each scope's slow mode;
  - `Gate::check()`: a message refused for a restriction, the slow mode, the rate
    (`moderation.rate`) or the filter, or let through filtered;
  - `Filter`: listed words masked or refused, links allowed, masked or kept to some sites, and
    markup that would run refused;
  - `Reports`, filed once per reporter with what was said as it was, then resolved or dismissed;
  - and a log of all of it. Its tables are personal data.
- With `conversations` too, every message and edit in a conversation goes through moderation in the
  scope `conversation:{id}`, and the moderators — a conversation's owner, or whoever has
  `admin.chat.moderate` — have `/mute`, `/unmute`, `/ban`, `/unban`, `/kick` and `/slow`.

### Documentation

- Moderation Guide, new.
- Conversations guide: *Moderation*.

## Audience: the segments and groups list is a datatable

### Changed

- `/admin/Segments` lists segments and groups in a server-side datatable (FW-259). Its rows come
  from the new `Segments/data`: the name with the description, the kind, the members, who entered
  and who left in seven days, what uses it, the owner, and when it was last worked out. The most
  members come first. It sorts by any column but *used by*, searches name and description, pages,
  and copies or exports. The tabs stay above it with their counts and are sent as the filter. What
  uses a segment is worked out for the rows shown only.
- `/admin/Groups` lists groups the same way, from the new `Groups/data`, the most members first.
  The members are counted in one query, where the list counted them one group at a time.

### Added

- `Html\Datatable\ArraySource::answer()`: a server-side datatable's answer from rows in memory,
  for a list that is not one table's rows. The cells are drawn for the page alone.
- `Audience\Segments::movements()`: every segment's week of entries and departures, in two
  queries.

### Documentation

- HTML Components guide: *A list that is not one table's rows — `ArraySource`*.
- Audience guide: *The screens*. Authorization guide: *The screen* (groups).

## Groups and segments: a tab each, and what a group needs to replace an application's own

  account's own, groups first, each with its own datatable — columns, picker, export name — and
  no *All* tab or *kind* column. Groups show their number, members, order and what uses them,

- `Controllers\Groups::all()` (every group with its members, in one query) and `membersUrl()`.

## Segments: the first members are the start, not people entering

### Fixed

- A segment's *entered, 7 days* counted its first members (FW-264): the first working out writes
  everybody in at once, with that moment as their `entered_at`, so a segment made this week showed
  as many entered as it had members. The migration `add_first_computed_at_to_segments` adds
  `segments.firstcomputedat` — set by the first working out, and for segments already worked out
  dated by their earliest member — and movement counts only what came after it: in
  `Segments::movement()` and `movements()`, the library, Ask's `last7days`, and the rule tester's
  entries into and departures from a segment. A segment younger than a week shows *since DD/MM*
  beside its figures.

### Added

- `Segments::countsFrom()`, `Segments::knowsItsStart()`, and `firstComputedAt` on a segment.

### Documentation

- Audience guide: *Segments*. Upgrade Guide: a row.

## Auth: `impersonations` holds only the ways back still open

### Changed

- `impersonations` kept every impersonation for good, with how it ended — a second history beside
  the audit log and the activity log. It now holds only the ways back still open: a row is deleted
  when it is used, signed out of or refused, and those past their expiry when the next begins. The
  audit log records `user.impersonation_started` and `user.impersonation_ended` with how;
  `ended_at` and `ended_by` are gone from the creating migration. An installation that already ran
  it applies the one-time statement in the Upgrade Guide; no migration carries it.

### Fixed

- `AuditLog::query()` returned a fatal error where a backend answered a failed query with false
  instead of throwing; it answers no rows.

### Documentation

- Authentication guide: *Signing in as somebody else, and back*. Upgrade Guide: a row.

## History in the changelog, not in tables of its own

### Changed

- Who joined and who left a group is written to the changelog — `group.joined` / `group.left` on
  the group, entity `usergroup` — where the library counts it; `usergroup_log` is gone from the
  migration `add_membership_history_to_usergroups`, and `Groups::forget()` with it. The
  membership's own `addedat`, `addedby` and `addedsource` stay: they are state, gone with it.
- What a message said before an edit goes to the change feed, entity `conversation_message`, kept
  as long as the changelog keeps diffs; `conversation_message_edits` is gone from
  `create_conversations_tables`. `Messages::edits()` reads it back.
- What moderation did is written to the audit log as `moderation.{action}` — about the subject,
  in the scope — and `Moderation::history()` reads it from there; `moderation_log` is gone from
  `create_moderation_tables`. `AuditLog::about()` records an event about a target that is not an
  account, and `AuditLog::query()` filters by `target_type` / `target_id`.
- A dashboard's layout is a user setting, `dashboard.{area}` in `usersettings`, where
  `user_dashboards` kept it: the migration that made that table is gone.
- Account erasure deletes the account's `usersettings` rows. It left them, though they are the
  person's own.
- None needs a migration of its own: the creating migrations no longer make the tables, and
  an installation that ran them drops them once, by hand (Upgrade Guide). The group movements and
  a message's earlier versions need the `changelog` feature to keep anything.
- CLAUDE.md rule 14: history goes in the framework's logs, and a framework migration that has run
  is changed in place, with a one-time statement, never undone by a second one.

### Documentation

- Audience guide: *Groups*. Conversations guide: *Messages*, *Storage*. Moderation guide: *The
  log*, *Storage*. Dashboard guide: *Layouts*. Authentication guide: account erasure. Upgrade Guide:
  four rows.

## The framework's own tables back in `pramnos` and `authserver`

### Changed

- Until October the framework kept its own tables out of the application's: the engine's in
  `pramnos` (the changelog, email tracking, opt-outs, push), the sign-in's in `authserver`. The
  tables added since went into the application's schema. They are back where they belong:
  `authserver.remember_tokens`, `authserver.impersonations`, and in `pramnos` the client vitals,
  the scheduler's runs and overrides, the queue's idempotency keys, the event stream
  (`event_log`, `event_consumers`, `event_deliveries`), mail bodies, and the audience engine's
  state — `rule_log`, `rule_runs`, `segment_members`, `person_events`, `experiment_assignments`.
  On MySQL the schema is a name prefix (`pramnos_rule_log`). What the application defines and
  manages — groups, segments, rules, consent lists, experiments, conversations, the assistant's —
  stays in its schema, and so do tables the framework adopted from applications that had them
  first: `javascripterrors`, `bounces`, `llm_calls`.
- The creating migrations make the tables in their schema; an installation that ran them moves
  them once by hand (Upgrade Guide). Verified on TimescaleDB that a hypertable with compressed
  chunks moves with them.
- CLAUDE.md rule 15: a new framework table goes in `pramnos` or `authserver`, unless it is the
  application's own data or a table applications already have under that name.

### Documentation

- The guides name the tables where they now are. Upgrade Guide: a row.

## Live conversations: the daemon reads their channels, and a refused one is caught up

### Fixed

- `broadcast:serve` ingests `private-conversation.*` by itself where the `conversations` feature is
  on and the backplane is Redis pub/sub (FW-268), as it does every user's notifications channel.
  It read only `private-users.*` unless told, so what a worker wrote to a conversation — the
  analyst's answer — never reached an open page. On `redis-stream`, which no pattern reads, it
  says so at startup.
- The dock catches a conversation up every few seconds while the server has not confirmed its
  channel — the auth endpoint refusing it, the socket down — where it stayed "connected" and
  received nothing.

### Documentation

- Conversations guide: *Live: what browsers are told* — the three things that make the
  conversations live: the ingest, the auth endpoint's rules (`Realtime::authorize()` for an auth
  controller of the application's own), client events.
