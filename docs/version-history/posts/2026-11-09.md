## The New report form shows the report's name and sections; a view variable named like the view warns

### Fixed

- **`MetricReports/edit`** showed "metricreports" in the Name field and "Array" in Sections and
  rows. The controller passed `name` and `sections` to the view, and inside a template
  `$this->name` and `$this->sections` read the view's own properties (its name, its template
  sections), not the variables. They are now `reportName` and `sectionsText`, in all three themes.
  The screen's test now draws the templates inside a real `View`, so the same collision fails it.

### Changed

- **`View::__set()` warns** (`E_USER_WARNING`) when a variable has the name of a property declared
  on `View` (`name`, `path`, `type`, `sections`, …). The variable is still kept for a controller
  that reads it back. See the Theme guide, *Template Variables*, and the Upgrade Guide row.
- The `pramnos_check` rule `view-reserved-props` also finds `name` and `type`.

## Many cards of one dashboard widget

### Added

- **`Widget::repeatable()`**: a board may hold many cards of such a widget, each kept as
  `<id>~<key>` with its own settings and width. The catalogue keeps offering the widget after one
  is placed, and each "Add" makes a new card. `WidgetRegistry::forCard()` and `Widget::baseId()`
  resolve a card to its widget, and the dashboard controller, `Layouts::clean()` and
  `dashboard:precompute` use them. This is the ground for the reports' widgets: several tables
  and charts on one report. See the Dashboard guide, *Many cards of one widget*.

## Reports are boards of cards, arranged with the dashboard's editor

### Added

- **A report is a board** (`Pramnos\Metrics\ReportBoard`, board `report:<id>`), arranged on its
  own page with the dashboard's editor: Customise, drag, resize, tabs, the card form, and every
  change drawn at once with real numbers. Only the owner arranges it; everybody else copies it.
- **Report cards** (`Pramnos\Metrics\Widgets`): section (a heading that folds the cards under it),
  table of metrics (rows across the report's columns or its own), chart (series over N periods,
  the year before beside them), figure (a value and its change), text (markdown), and records (the
  `rows()` behind a metric). Each reads the report's grain, period, comparison and columns.
- **Card settings that keep a structure**: `textarea`, `list` (items added, removed and dragged
  into order, with selects that follow another field) and `filters` (a dimension and its value),
  drawn by `window.pfStructured` in `pf-utils.js`. A card's form now picks metrics, breakdowns and
  filters from the catalogue instead of taking syntax.
- **Charts in a mailed report are PNG images** carried inside the mail: `ChartImage::svg()` draws
  them on the server, and Imagick or `rsvg-convert` makes the PNG. Without either, the chart is
  its numbers and the mail still goes. `Email::addInlineImageData()` carries an image the HTML
  shows by `cid:`.

### Changed

- A report's `sections` are read as cards: a heading, its chart, its table. Existing saved reports
  and those in app.php show as before and can be arranged at once. The report's settings form
  keeps the whole report as text, for the advanced, in place of its cards.
- The CSV of a report holds its tables one under another.
- **How a report reads.** A card is headed by the heading it was given, or by nothing; the kind's
  name ("Table of metrics") is for the catalogue only. A section is a thin line with a fold arrow,
  not a card. Cards and table rows are denser. Customise sits in the report's own bar, beside
  Settings. A card's hidden tools stay hidden under an administration's older Bootstrap.

See the Metrics guide (*Reports*), the Dashboard guide (*Settings that keep a structure*, *A
report's board*) and the Upgrade Guide row.

## Metrics and BI: a design for every application, for approval

### Documentation

- **[Metrics and BI — design](../../Metrics-BI-Design.md)**: the framework's statistics and BI as
  one engine for every application, built from the needs of six applications and of small
  projects on MySQL:
  - an application declares its metrics, and nothing else;
  - the store moves to `timestamptz`, `jsonb` and a tenant on every row;
  - hour grains, more aggregations and late data;
  - declared sources the framework can turn into `time_bucket` queries and continuous aggregates;
  - live metrics for per-entity series;
  - all of TimescaleDB where it is present;
  - one query API for cards, reports, the JSON API and the assistant;
  - alerts, goals and annotations;
  - how each application's own mechanism moves over.

  Whether values go in one hypertable or one per grain is decided on measurements in three sizes
  (phase 0). The design is a proposal: no code is written against it before it is approved.

## Metrics and BI: the storage measured, and a hypertable per grain chosen

### Documentation

- **[Metrics and BI — design](../../Metrics-BI-Design.md)** is approved, with the owner's
  decisions in §13 and ideas from other BI systems in §14.
- Phase 0 is done. Its measurements are in the new §5.4:
  - **Sizes and engines.** The three storage layouts were measured on 78 k, 15.6 M and 99 M
    rows, on TimescaleDB 2.31 with the Toolkit and on plain PostgreSQL 18. The small size was
    also run on PostgreSQL 10.
  - **The choice: B, a hypertable per grain, on every database.** At 99 M rows a 24-month chart
    takes 0.5 ms in B and 3.1 ms in A, a single table. Once compressed, A takes 10 ms. B is also
    the smallest on disk and the fastest to recompute.
  - **Compression and closed months.** Compression is 7–9×. Rewriting a compressed closed month
    is cheap enough to do inline on 2.31, but a month of hours goes past TimescaleDB's default
    limit on decompressed tuples per DML. The recomputation lifts that limit for its own
    transaction.
  - **Distinct counts.** A distinct count from `hyperloglog(4096)` sketches reads in 2–4 ms,
    where counting exactly takes 0.2–9 s, with an error under 3 %. 4096 buckets is the default.
    A figure compared with a ledger, such as paying customers, is declared exact.
  - **Real data.** On a marketplace's real invoices, one `time_bucket` query over a unix-time
    column is 5× faster than computing day by day. A continuous aggregate of it reads a year in
    1.6 ms.
- The scripts are in `benchmarks/metrics-storage/`, which is not part of the package.

## A copied report with Greek names is no longer saved empty

### Fixed

- **Splitting text into lines no longer cuts Greek letters in half.** Copying a report saved it
  with an empty definition.
  - **Cause.** The report's text was split into lines with `\R`, without the `u` flag. Without
    it, `\R` also matches the byte 0x85, and that byte is the second half of «υ» (`cf 85`) and of
    other Greek letters. Every such letter was split, the definition held invalid UTF-8,
    `json_encode` failed, and the store wrote `''`.
  - **The fix.** Every line split in the framework now names its line endings (`\r\n|\r|\n`).
    This is byte-safe, and unlike the `u` flag it does not give up on a whole text because one
    byte in it is invalid. The same change is in:
    - a report's sections and columns;
    - the changes the assistant reads;
    - the AI top-ups list and its settings screen;
    - the documentation tool's front matter and search;
    - `pramnos:check`;
    - the schema diff;
    - project setup.
  - **Saving.** `Reports::save()` and `saveLayout()` now throw a `JsonException` when a
    definition does not encode, instead of storing it empty.

## Reports: a new one opens to be arranged, columns line up in every theme

### Fixed

- **Columns.** A report's values and their headings are both aligned right, inline. A theme that
  aligned only the numbers made every heading read as the next column's, with a last column
  left unheaded.
- **The catalogue names each kind of card.** A repeatable card was offered under the heading of
  the last card drawn: the section kind showed as the report's last section.
- **One heading per section.** A chart read from a report's sections no longer repeats its
  section's heading under it.
- **List settings.** The words of a list setting's fields (Label, Metric, Filters, …) are
  translated. Each item is a fixed grid, one column per field, so items no longer wrap
  differently from each other.
- **Values of a dimension.** Values with invalid UTF-8 in a metric's dimensions are substituted
  instead of silently emptying the dimensions' JSON and their hash.

### Changed

- **New report.** It asks for a name, a period, a comparison and who sees and gets it, then opens
  the empty report straight in Customise. The columns, the report as text and the list of
  metrics fold under *For the advanced*. A report of one's own with no cards opens in Customise
  (`data-pf-dashboard-open` on the Customise button). Its empty note says to add the first card.
- **Greek.** The framework's Greek catalogue calls a metric «δείκτης».

## Metrics and BI, phase 1: a table per grain, on TimescaleDB hypertables

### Changed

- **A table per grain.** The metrics' values are kept in `stats.metric_values_day` … `_year`.
  `stats.metric_values` becomes a view over them with a `grain` column. Each row:
  - has a `timestamptz` period, `jsonb` dimensions, a uuid hash and a `double precision` value;
  - has a `tenant` and a `version`, and an `extra` for what a value carries beside its number.
- **TimescaleDB.** Each table is a hypertable with its grain's chunk interval and is compressed
  by metric, tenant and breakdown. A closed period is rewritten inside a compressed chunk, with
  the decompression limit lifted for that transaction alone.
- **The migration** `2026_11_06_000001` is changed in place. An installation that ran its
  single-table form calls `Store::ensure($db)` once, which converts it and keeps every value.

### Added

- **`Pramnos\Metrics\Store`.**
  - `ensure()` makes the tables, the view and the hypertables, and is safe to run again.
    `drop()` removes them.
  - `table($grain)` names a grain's table.
  - `adopt()` imports an application's own store or statistics tables once, by a map of its
    columns. A `version` in the map keeps another definition's figures beside version 1, which
    is the version computed and read.
  - `lock()` and `unlock()` take and release the store's advisory lock.
- **`QueryBuilder::upsertMany()`**: `upsert()` for a list, one statement per 500 rows.
- **The store is made on first use.** The first computation or read in a process checks the
  grains' tables once (`Store::ready()`). Where one is missing, or the single table of before is
  still there, it runs `ensure()` under the lock. A deploy whose migration has not run yet does not
  fail on a missing table.
- **The store's lock.** While a conversion or an import holds the lock, `Metrics::compute()`
  reports `busy` and does nothing; `metrics:compute` exits 0, and the next run computes what is
  due.

### Fixed

- **`Store::adopt()` and the conversion read their table once, row by row**, and write a
  thousand rows a statement. They used to page with `ORDER BY period LIMIT … OFFSET`. Many rows
  share a period, so the order between pages was not stable, and rows could be skipped or
  read twice without any error.
- **Reading a metric asks the database for the breakdown it needs**, and for the total that marks
  which periods were computed. It used to fetch every breakdown and keep one in PHP. On MySQL a
  year of days now reads in 13 ms instead of 138 ms, and a 30×12 report in 39 ms instead of
  168 ms (the design's §5.4).
- **`SchemaBuilder::renameTable()` on PostgreSQL** renames a table inside a schema. It used to
  give `RENAME TO` a qualified name, which is a syntax error.

See the Metrics guide (*Where the values are kept*), the design's §5.1 and §12, and the Upgrade
Guide row.

## A conversation's message is notified in the client its reader is in

### Added

- **Notifications follow the person, as in Slack, Teams and Discord.** A message's push and its
  realtime notification go to the client its reader was last active in: a tab, or the desktop
  application. When the reader has been away longer than they chose (0, 5, 10 or 30 minutes; 5 by
  default), they go to every client. The bell is told of every message, always.
  - **Each tab's id.** `window.pfClientId` in pf-utils.js, kept in sessionStorage.
  - **Activity reports.** `window.pfActivity()` reports to the conversations' new `active`,
    starting by itself where the dock is. `away` reads or sets the minutes.
  - **On the server.** `Pramnos\Conversations\Clients` keeps one row per person in
    `pramnos.conversation_clients` and decides: `target()`, `active()`, `awayAfter()`.
  - **The push.** It goes to that tab's subscription. `pushsubscriptions.client` is filled by
    `Subscriptions::tagClient()` from the subscribe request's `client`, which the push stub now
    sends. A subscription that names no client is told as before. A notification names its client
    with `pushClient()`.
  - **The realtime telling.** `conversation.activity` carries `notify`: a client's id, or `all`.
- **Two migrations:** `pramnos.conversation_clients`, and `pushsubscriptions.client`.

See the Conversations guide (*Told in the client the person is in*) and the Push guide (*A push to
one client*).

## Metrics and BI, phase 2: one query for every reader, and figures per organisation

### Added

- **`Metrics::query()`** (`Pramnos\Metrics\Query`) is the one read for cards, reports, exports,
  the JSON API and the assistant:
  - **the read:** `grain()`, `between()` or `at()`, `by()`, `where()` and `compare()`;
  - **the viewer:** `for($user)` refuses a metric the viewer may not read and narrows the values
    to their organisations; `tenant($id)` narrows to one;
  - **the answers:** `series()`, `value()`, `compared()`, `table()` and `top($n)`.

  `Metrics::series()`, `value()` and `compare()` are now short forms of it, with their signatures
  unchanged. The framework's readers read through it as their viewer:
  - a report's cells and KPI cards, as the person the report is built for;
  - a metric card, as the person the board is drawn for;
  - the assistant's metrics tool, as the administrator asking.
- **Figures per organisation.** `Metric::tenant('organizationid')` keeps each value per
  organisation: the source is asked by that column too, and each organisation's total is its own
  breakdown added up. `metrics.tenant` in app.php says which organisations a viewer may read.
  Reading a distinct metric across organisations throws, because it does not add up.
- **By the hour.**
  - `Window::HOUR`: the zone's hour, counted in time, so the night the clocks go back has 25.
  - `Metric::grains(['hour', 'day', …])` keeps a metric by the hour. No other metric is, since
    `Window::DEFAULT_GRAINS` stays day to year.
  - A new migration makes `stats.metric_values_hour`, a compressed hypertable with 7-day chunks.
  - `metrics.retention` (`['hour' => '90 days']`) drops older values after each computation.
  - `--from`/`--to` days cover every hour of the last day.

## A deploy no longer drops every realtime connection

### Fixed

- **A deploy no longer restarts the WebSocket server.** A new commit or a migration restarted every
  daemon, and with it `broadcast:serve`, so every open chat lost its connection. The client found
  out only after a reconnect backoff of up to 30 s, or after the 120-second activity timeout when
  the connection looked open but was dead. A day of deploys meant as many interruptions.
  - The orchestrator now leaves the server running while its own code is unchanged. Its
    fingerprint covers the framework's broadcasting code, the `broadcast:serve` command and
    `app/app.php`.
  - A daemon of the application's own can ask for the same with `'restartOnDeploy' => false` and
    a `'fingerprint'`.
- **A restart that does happen is gentle.** The server tells every connected client
  `pusher:error` 4200, "reconnect now", before closing. pf-utils reconnects within a second
  instead of after its backoff, and re-reads what it missed once.

## A group conversation has a name, and its members rename it

### Added

- **A group conversation may be named.** Groups with the same people could not be told apart, and
  somebody wrote in a group believing it was a direct conversation. A group's title used to be its
  members even when it had a name.
  - Its name, up to 80 characters, is now its title. The sidebar also gives `membersTitle`, so a
    search finds it by its people.
  - `Conversations::rename($id, $name, $by)` (`rename/{id}`) lets any member rename it, or take
    its name away with an empty one. Somebody not in it gets 403.
  - A rename leaves a line in the conversation, shown at once and notifying nobody (the new
    `notice` option of `Messages::post()`). Members' sidebars get `conversation.changed` with the
    `name`, and the changelog keeps `conversation.renamed`.
  - A direct conversation keeps no name.

## A conversation's push is quiet, and none while the site is in front

### Changed

- **A conversation's push now plays no system sound** (`silent`). The chat page and the desktop
  application play the conversations' own sound.
- **No push while a page of the site is visible and focused** (`unlessFocused`). With the chat
  open in front, the reader used to get two notifications, the page's and the push.
- **Where the flags live.** Both are flags of `toPush()` that `PushChannel` passes on only when
  set, and both workers honour them: `pf-push-worker.js` and the `sw.js` stub. A notification
  that asks for neither, such as a sign-in approval, is shown as before.

## The metrics' one-off conversion is taken out

### Changed

- **The conversion is gone.** `Store::ensure()` and `Store::ready()` no longer convert a single
  `stats.metric_values` table of an earlier version. That table existed in one installation only,
  which has been converted. They now make only what is missing: the grains' tables and the view.
  `Store::adopt()` stays, for an application's own figures.

## Metrics and BI, phase 2 done: each organisation's own day

### Added

- **A time zone per organisation.** `metrics.timezone` may be a function of the organisation, with
  `metrics.tenants` listing them and `metrics.default_timezone` the installation's zone. A metric
  with `tenant()` is then computed once per zone, in that zone's window, and kept for its
  organisations. Each organisation has a total for every period, nought where it had nothing.
- **Reading.** Reads find each organisation's periods by its own zone (`Window::zoneOf()`), and
  several organisations together add up each one's own day. With this, phase 2 of the design is
  done.

## Metrics and BI, phase 3: a metric's query declared, not written

### Added

- **A declared source (§4.4).** Instead of a closure, a metric can declare its query, and the
  framework writes it for every period, grouped by the dimensions asked, with the tenant column
  where there is one:
  - the table: `from()`;
  - the time column: `at($column, unix: true)`, which reads a unix-time column as it is;
  - what is counted: `sum()`, `count()` or `countDistinct()`, the last making the metric distinct;
  - the conditions: `where()`;
  - each dimension's column or expression: `dimension($name, $column)`.

  This is the ground for one `time_bucket` query per grain and continuous aggregates, which come
  next.
- **Gaps (§4.6, §4.7).**
  - `Metric::since($day)` and `Metric::missing(fn (Window $w): ?string)` say which periods the
    source cannot answer. Such a period is not computed, so it reads as no figure.
  - `Metrics::query()->gaps()` gives the reasons, and a report's cell shows the reason, not a
    nought.
  - `Metric::expect('1 hour')` says how often data should arrive.
- **A mean, a least and a most (§4.3).**
  - `Metric::AVG`, `MIN` and `MAX`, declared with `avg()`, `min()` and `max()`. A mean is kept
    with how many it is of, so means across organisations combine weighted.
  - `Metric::direction()` (higher or lower is better) and `Metric::format()` (number, currency,
    percent or duration) say how a value is presented.
- **Many periods in one query (§4.4).** Computing many periods of a declared unix-time source on
  PostgreSQL reads them grouped by period in the metrics' zone, one query per breakdown, instead
  of one query per period. A year of days of real invoices took 28 ms instead of 152 ms in
  phase 0's measurement. Other databases and sources keep one query per period.
- **Data that arrive late (§6.3).** `Metric::lateness('7 days')` keeps an ended period open, and
  computes it again at each run, until its lateness has passed. Only then does it close. Without
  it, a period closes at its end, as before.
- **A meter and a latest reading (§4.3).** A declared source can now count two more ways:
  - `last($column)`: the latest reading of the period;
  - `counter($column, per: $entity)`: the increase of a cumulative meter, per entity and added up,
    a fall counted as a reset.
- **Cohorts (§4.6).** `Metric::cohort()->from()->of()->event()->within([7, 14, 30])` counts, of
  those who began in a period, how many reached an event within each number of days. It is kept
  per cohort period with the dimension `within`, and its total is the cohort's size.
- **Live metrics (§4.5).** `Metric::live()` reads a metric from its source when asked and never
  stores it: any breakdown of its dimensions, filtered, by the periods asked. Computing passes it
  by. On TimescaleDB, a live metric over a hypertable gets an hourly continuous aggregate
  (`stats.live_<name>`, with a refresh policy and real-time reads), made once by `metrics:compute`,
  and is read from it.
- **To the application's own list (§4.6).** `Metric::drill(fn (Window $w, array $filters): string)`
  links a report's number to the application's own list, filtered, instead of the framework's
  records. A cell's link carries it as `url`.
- **Versions (§4.6).** `Metric::version(2)` computes and reads a corrected definition.
  `Query::version(1)` reads the figures of the one before, kept beside it and never touched by a
  recomputation.

## Metrics and BI, phase 4: goals that follow a curve

### Added

- **Goals (§9.2).** `Pramnos\Metrics\Goals` keeps a target per metric, day or month, period,
  organisation and filters, in `stats.goals`, made by a new migration.
  - **The expected share.** `Goals::progress()` gives the actual so far against the expected
    share. That share is the curve of the same weekday a year earlier, or of the last four same
    weekdays, scaled to the target, with a forecast along the same curve.
  - **What it reads.** A day's goal reads the hours completed, and a month's goal the days.
  - **A hole in the reference** gives no answer, so it never says something false.
- **Alerts (§9.1).** `Pramnos\Metrics\Alerts` keeps rules in `stats.alert_rules`, made by a new
  migration.
  - **What they watch:** a threshold, a change against the period before or a year earlier, or data
    that stopped, meaning nought for the last N seconds of completed hours. A rule can be limited to
    hours of the day.
  - **Who is told:** a firing goes to its users' bells and pushes and to its conversation, then
    waits before it may fire again. It is kept in the changelog as `alert.fired`.
  - **When:** `metrics:compute` checks the rules after each run.
- **Notes on the charts (§9.3).** `Pramnos\Metrics\Annotations` keeps notes in
  `stats.annotations`, made by a new migration: a moment or a span, an organisation's or
  everybody's, with words, a kind and a link.
  - **Drawing.** Report charts and metric cards draw them as dashed lines at their period, through
    `Html\Chart::render()`'s new `annotations` option, and list them under the chart.
  - **Deploys.** The daemon orchestrator notes each deploy it notices.

  With this, phase 4 is done.
